\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate mm3DGS on six outdoor ColoRadar~\cite{kramer2022coloradar} scenes
captured with a TI MMWCAS cascaded mmWave radar (12 transmitters, 16
receivers, 76 GHz carrier, 5.93 cm range resolution) and a co-registered
Ouster OS1-64 LiDAR. The scenes span structured warehouse interiors,
parking lots, and outdoor staging areas with both static infrastructure
(walls, vehicles, traffic infrastructure) and limited dynamic content.

% --------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:experiments:setup}

\textbf{Scene splits.} For each of the six benchmark scenes we select 9
adjacent cascaded radar frames captured at 5\,Hz (200~ms inter-frame
spacing). The middle frame $F$ is held out as the novel-view test frame;
the eight bracketing frames $\{F\!-\!4, \ldots, F\!-\!1, F\!+\!1, \ldots,
F\!+\!4\}$ form the training set. We use only the first chirp loop
(``chirp 0'') of each frame, giving 8 training RA maps and 1 held-out
test RA map per scene. Restricting evaluation to the temporally
\emph{interpolated} test frame (rather than extrapolating to the
trajectory edge) is the standard NVS protocol; trajectory edges introduce
free-view extrapolation error that is unrelated to the radar physics
under test. The test pose is reconstructed via per-loop interpolation
between the held-out test frame's neighbours, never read from the test
frame's own ground-truth alignment, so the test pose is itself
NVS-inferred.

\textbf{Compared methods.} We benchmark mm3DGS against three published
radar NVS baselines using public reference implementations adapted to
our 9-frame split (per-method adapter details in Appendix~\ref{app:baseline_adapters}):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{DART}~\cite{huang2024dart}: NeRF-style range-Doppler
      learning with ego-motion compensation. We use the cascaded variant
      (``DART (cascaded)''), which renders against the same radar
      configuration as our method.
\item \textbf{Radar Fields}~\cite{10.1145/3641519.3657510}: power-only
      frequency-space neural field for range--azimuth response.
\item \textbf{RadarSplat}~\cite{kung2025radarsplat}: 3DGS-derived
      anisotropic Gaussian splatting for power-only RA rendering.
\end{itemize}
All baselines render power (magnitude) RA maps; comparisons are therefore
performed on $|\mathrm{RA}|$ even though mm3DGS produces complex range
profiles.

\textbf{Metrics.} Following Kramer et al.~\cite{kramer2022coloradar} and
mmIR~\cite{mmIR2026}, the primary metric is the Pearson correlation
coefficient (CC) between the rendered $|\mathrm{RA}|_{\text{cart}}$ and
ground-truth $|\mathrm{RA}|_{\text{cart}}$ on a $399 \times 399$
Cartesian grid (each pixel sampled from the polar render via bilinear
$F.grid\_sample$). The Cartesian render uses the same range/azimuth bin
bounds, range resolution, and azimuth-arcsin geometry as the polar
output; range bins 15--110 are retained and the central wedge is masked
to the radar's actual azimuth FOV (matching the standard ColoRadar
evaluation).

\textbf{Implementation.} mm3DGS uses 20{,}000 oriented points per scene
with per-point ITU material vectors and quaternion-encoded normals,
trained for 500 iterations of Adam ($\eta_{\text{mat}}\!=\!10^{-2}$,
$\eta_{\text{rot}}\!=\!5\!\cdot\!10^{-3}$, $\eta_{\text{pos}}\!=\!10^{-5}$
with L2 anchor $\lambda_{\text{pos}}\!=\!100$). Adaptive density control
(Sec.~\ref{sec:methods:opt}) splits the top 5\% of points by accumulated
amplitude-path position gradient and prunes the bottom 5\% at iters
$\{100, 200, 300, 400\}$. Each scene completes in $\sim$2.3 minutes on a
single NVIDIA RTX~4090.

\subsection{Held-out novel-view |RA| reconstruction}
\label{sec:experiments:test}

Table~\ref{tab:test_ra} reports per-scene held-out test $|\mathrm{RA}|$
metrics for mm3DGS and the three baselines: Pearson correlation (Corr),
peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR), structural similarity (SSIM), and
root-mean-square error (RMSE), all computed on min-max-normalized
399$\times$399 Cartesian $|\mathrm{RA}|$ images via the same harness
(\texttt{mmir.evaluation.utils.metrics.compute\_cart\_ra\_metrics}) used
by all four methods. mm3DGS \textbf{wins on every scene} for Corr and
SSIM and on \textbf{the 6-scene mean for all four metrics}:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{Corr}: mm3DGS \textbf{0.600} vs.\ RadarSplat 0.339,
      Radar Fields 0.120, DART 0.099 ($1.8\times$ the next-best).
\item \textbf{PSNR}: mm3DGS \textbf{28.6 dB} vs.\ Radar Fields 28.0,
      RadarSplat 25.6, DART 22.6 (+0.6 dB over Radar Fields, +6.0 dB
      over DART).
\item \textbf{SSIM}: mm3DGS \textbf{0.729} vs.\ RadarSplat 0.523,
      Radar Fields 0.495, DART 0.410 ($1.4\times$ the next-best).
\item \textbf{RMSE}: mm3DGS \textbf{0.038} vs.\ Radar Fields 0.040,
      RadarSplat 0.056, DART 0.076.
\end{itemize}

The improvement is consistent across all six scenes, including the
scene with the lowest absolute correlation (\texttt{S2\,F300}, where
mm3DGS achieves Corr 0.508 vs.\ Radar Fields 0.172). Three ablations
clarify this margin:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Why mm3DGS beats RadarSplat.} RadarSplat uses opaque
      per-Gaussian features without a physics-based BSDF. Its renderer
      cannot exploit the directional dependence of the bistatic return
      (cosine-of-incidence falloff, antenna gain interaction with surface
      normal), so its model has to absorb this physics implicitly into
      learned features. With only 8 training frames per scene, the
      learned features over-fit per-pose appearance and fail to
      interpolate to the held-out test pose.
\item \textbf{Why mm3DGS beats Radar Fields and DART.} Both methods
      operate in radar-image space rather than the underlying complex
      signal space. They cannot disentangle FFT windowing artifacts,
      antenna sidelobes, and material physics from the rendered image,
      so their fitted scene parameters partially encode sensor-specific
      processing. When evaluated on the held-out test pose, this
      sensor-specific overhead penalises generalisation.
\item \textbf{Why mm3DGS does not exceed 0.6 mean.} The 5\,Hz cascade
      capture rate fundamentally limits the multi-frame coherence
      available for NVS. We measured the inter-frame coherence ceiling
      empirically: the Pearson correlation between $|\mathrm{RA}|_F$ and
      $|\mathrm{RA}|_{F\pm1}$ (the closest training neighbours of the
      test frame) averages 0.586 across the six scenes
      (Appendix~\ref{app:upper_bounds}). This ``F$\pm$1 single-NN
      copy'' ceiling represents the maximum achievable correlation by
      \emph{any} method that, conceptually, predicts the test frame as a
      copy of the nearest training frame. mm3DGS's 0.60 mean lies
      slightly above this ceiling, indicating that the model captures
      multi-frame structure beyond a 1-NN copy. Capture rates of 10\,Hz
      or higher (faster cascade radars) would relax this
      ceiling considerably.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Qualitative comparison.} Figure~\ref{fig:training_ra} shows
held-out test $|\mathrm{RA}|$ renders for all four methods on the six
scenes, sorted left-to-right by mm3DGS test CC (descending). mm3DGS
preserves dominant scene structure (walls, parked vehicles, support
columns) with consistent range and azimuth localization across scenes,
while RadarSplat tends to produce over-smoothed wedges, and DART /
Radar Fields exhibit substantial range bin smearing because their
implicit representations cannot retain sharp range-bin support without
explicit phase modelling.

\subsection{Training-view fit}
\label{sec:experiments:train}

While the held-out novel-view metrics are the primary NVS measurement,
training-view fit quantifies how well the model captures the radar
physics in the regime where it has full supervision.
Table~\ref{tab:train_ra} reports mm3DGS's per-train-frame Pearson
correlation, PSNR, SSIM, and RMSE, computed via the same Cartesian
$|\mathrm{RA}|$ harness as Table~\ref{tab:test_ra} but averaged across
the 8 training frames per scene (mean$\pm$std). The training-mean
Corr of $\sim$0.81 is substantially higher than the held-out test
Corr of $\sim$0.60, reflecting (i) the expected gap between in-sample
and out-of-sample performance and (ii) the inter-frame coherence
ceiling discussed above. The init-test CC column reports the test
correlation \emph{before any optimization}, using only the
LiDAR-derived geometry with default ITU concrete materials. The
optimization recovers $\sim$0.20--0.30 Corr of additional test
correlation beyond geometric initialization, confirming that the
learned per-point material parameters carry substantial radar-physics
information. The same trend holds across all three baselines: their
\emph{train}-view Corr means are 0.371 (RadarSplat), 0.129 (Radar
Fields), and 0.033 (DART) --- all substantially below mm3DGS's 0.812
--- indicating that none of the three baselines fit the radar physics
on the training views as well as mm3DGS does, even before considering
their much larger held-out generalisation gap.

\subsection{Negative results and ablation summary}
\label{sec:experiments:ablations}

We report two classes of negative findings whose details are deferred
to Appendix~\ref{app:ablations}, but whose summary is informative.

\textbf{Initialization variants.} We tested 11 alternative
initialization strategies, including union-of-amplitude scoring across
all train poses (A2--A4), LiDAR-intensity-weighted sampling (A4--A5),
strict-AND visibility filtering (B1, B2 with and without test pose),
voxel-aware proportional allocation (C1, C1b), and motion-compensated
RA-signal modulation (C2). All variants converge within MC noise of the
M0 default (cosine-importance + farthest-point sampling on a tightened
azimuth FOV cone matching the arcsin-spaced bin grid), with no variant
exceeding the F$\pm$1 inter-frame coherence ceiling. We conclude that
\emph{initialization} is not the lever for breaking this ceiling.

\textbf{Per-pose deformation MLPs.} We tested a pose-conditioned
per-Gaussian deformation MLP (MLP-A) — input pose 6-DoF + canonical
position, output per-point ($\Delta\mathbf{p}, \Delta\alpha$) — at three
configurations (raw 6D, strong L2 anchor, NeRF-style positional
encoding). All three converge to test CC within $\pm$0.03 of M0, with
positional encoding actually \emph{degrading} mean CC because the higher-
dimensional input space encourages train-pose memorization rather than
smooth interpolation across the 8 training poses. The conclusion: with
only 8 train poses bracketing 1 test pose, an MLP-based deformation
field cannot extract additional NVS structure beyond what the static
Gaussian point set already captures.

\textbf{What this implies.} Reaching higher novel-view CC will require
either (a) higher-rate radar capture ($\geq$10\,Hz cascade) where the
inter-frame coherence ceiling is naturally higher, or (b) on-support
test splits with denser bracketing of the test pose. We discuss these
directions in Sec.~\ref{sec:conclusion}.
